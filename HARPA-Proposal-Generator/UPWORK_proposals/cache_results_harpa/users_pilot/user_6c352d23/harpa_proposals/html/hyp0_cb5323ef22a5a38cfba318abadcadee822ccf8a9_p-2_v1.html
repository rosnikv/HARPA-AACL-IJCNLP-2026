Title

Enhancing scheduling optimization with block-sparse tensor networks and Generative Flow Networks.

Introduction

Problem Statement

Integrating block-sparse tensor networks with Generative Flow Networks will improve the diversity and quality of solutions for scheduling optimization tasks, as measured by the Quantum Inception Score.

Motivation

Existing methods for integrating Generative Flow Networks with U(1) symmetric tensor networks have primarily focused on constrained combinatorial optimization tasks using arbitrary integer-valued equality constraints. However, these approaches have not extensively explored the potential of leveraging tensor network structures to enhance the diversity of solutions in scheduling optimization tasks, particularly under complex constraints. This gap is significant because scheduling optimization is a critical problem in various industries, and improving solution diversity can lead to more robust and adaptable schedules. By integrating block-sparse tensor networks with Generative Flow Networks, this hypothesis aims to address this gap by enhancing both the diversity and quality of solutions in scheduling optimization tasks.

Proposed Method

This research explores the integration of block-sparse tensor networks with Generative Flow Networks to enhance the diversity and quality of solutions in scheduling optimization tasks. The hypothesis posits that by leveraging the block-sparse structure of tensor networks, which efficiently represent and generate diverse solutions, the combined approach will outperform traditional methods in generating robust and adaptable schedules. The integration focuses on encoding scheduling constraints directly into the tensor network, allowing the Generative Flow Network to explore a wider solution space while maintaining validity. This approach is expected to improve the Quantum Inception Score, a metric used to evaluate the quality and diversity of generated solutions. The research will involve implementing the integration using Python-based experiments, testing on benchmark scheduling tasks, and analyzing results across multiple runs. The expected outcome is a demonstrable improvement in both the diversity and quality of scheduling solutions, providing a novel approach to tackling complex scheduling problems.

Background

Block-Sparse Tensor Networks: Block-sparse tensor networks are used to efficiently represent and generate diverse solutions in constrained optimization tasks. In this experiment, they will be configured to encode scheduling constraints directly, leveraging U(1) global charge conservation to reduce space complexity and computational time. This approach allows for faster learning and convergence, ensuring that only necessary tensor components are updated. The block-sparse structure is expected to enhance the diversity of solutions by exploiting inherent symmetries in the data, making it particularly effective in generating novel solutions while maintaining constraints.

Generative Flow Networks: Generative Flow Networks (GFlowNets) are employed to generate compositional structures in a probabilistic manner, offering flexibility in solving diverse tasks such as scheduling optimization. In this experiment, GFlowNets will be used to sample solutions by incrementally adding entities onto current structures, with probabilities proportional to a target reward distribution. This method is effective for tasks like scheduling optimization, where it provides a powerful framework for generating diverse solutions. The integration with block-sparse tensor networks is expected to enhance the model's ability to explore the solution space efficiently.

Implementation

The proposed method involves integrating block-sparse tensor networks with Generative Flow Networks to enhance scheduling optimization tasks. The implementation begins with configuring block-sparse tensor networks to encode scheduling constraints directly, leveraging U(1) global charge conservation to reduce space complexity. This configuration ensures that the tensor network only generates valid solutions, reducing computational costs and improving learning efficiency. Next, Generative Flow Networks are employed to sample solutions by incrementally adding entities onto current structures, with probabilities proportional to a target reward distribution. The integration occurs by using the block-sparse tensor network to guide the sampling process of the GFlowNet, ensuring that only valid and diverse solutions are generated. The output from the tensor network serves as a constraint-aware guide for the GFlowNet, allowing it to explore a wider solution space while maintaining validity. The experiment will be conducted using Python-based experiments, with results analyzed across multiple runs to ensure robustness. The expected outcome is an improvement in both the diversity and quality of scheduling solutions, as measured by the Quantum Inception Score.
