Title

Integrate sub-trajectory balance with maximum entropy GFlowNets to enhance convergence and diversity in Bayesian inference.

Introduction

Problem Statement

Integrating sub-trajectory balance with maximum entropy GFlowNets will improve convergence speed and increase sample diversity in Bayesian network inference compared to using trajectory balance objectives alone.

Motivation

Current methods in GFlowNets often focus on either trajectory balance or local constraint-based learning independently, without exploring the potential synergy between specific sub-trajectory balance and maximum entropy objectives. Existing literature has not extensively tested the combination of sub-trajectory balance with maximum entropy GFlowNets to enhance exploration and improve convergence speed in Bayesian network inference. This hypothesis addresses the gap by integrating these two approaches to potentially yield more diverse and efficient sampling strategies.

Proposed Method

This research explores the integration of sub-trajectory balance with maximum entropy GFlowNets to enhance the efficiency and diversity of sampling in Bayesian network inference. The sub-trajectory balance focuses on partial trajectories, allowing for more granular credit assignment, while the maximum entropy objective ensures diverse exploration by fixing the backward policy as a uniform distribution. This combination is expected to improve convergence speed by leveraging the detailed exploration of sub-trajectories and the broad exploration encouraged by maximum entropy. The hypothesis will be tested by comparing the convergence speed and diversity of samples generated by this integrated approach against those produced by trajectory balance objectives alone. The experiment will utilize a Bayesian network inference task, where the diversity and speed of convergence are critical for accurate inference. The expected outcome is that the integrated approach will outperform the trajectory balance objective alone, providing a more efficient and diverse sampling strategy.

Background

Sub-Trajectory Balance: Sub-Trajectory Balance (SubTB) applies the balance condition to partial trajectories, improving credit assignment by focusing on intermediate states. This approach is particularly useful when full trajectory sampling is computationally expensive. In this experiment, SubTB will be implemented by defining partial subsequences within a trajectory and applying the balance condition to these subsequences. This allows for more granular control over the learning process, enabling the GFlowNet to adapt more quickly to changes in the reward landscape. The expected outcome is improved convergence speed due to better credit assignment.

Maximum Entropy GFlowNets: Maximum Entropy GFlowNets aim to maximize the entropy of the flow distribution, encouraging exploration of diverse trajectories. This is achieved by fixing the backward policy as a uniform distribution, leading to a unique global minimum of the trajectory balance objective that corresponds to a Markovian flow with maximum entropy. This approach is beneficial in scenarios where diversity of samples is crucial. In this experiment, maximum entropy will be used to ensure that the GFlowNet does not converge prematurely to suboptimal solutions, maintaining a broad exploration of the solution space. The expected outcome is increased sample diversity.

Implementation

The proposed method integrates sub-trajectory balance with maximum entropy GFlowNets to improve convergence speed and sample diversity in Bayesian network inference. The implementation involves defining partial subsequences within trajectories and applying the sub-trajectory balance condition to these subsequences. This is achieved by modifying the trajectory balance loss to account for partial trajectories, ensuring that the learned policy approximates the target distribution effectively. Concurrently, the maximum entropy objective is implemented by fixing the backward policy as a uniform distribution, which encourages diverse exploration. The integration occurs at the level of the loss function, where both sub-trajectory balance and maximum entropy terms are combined to guide the learning process. The experiment will be conducted using a Bayesian network inference task, where the GFlowNet will be trained to sample from the target distribution. The primary metric will be convergence speed, measured by the number of iterations required to reach a target accuracy. Sample diversity will be assessed by the entropy of the generated samples, reflecting the spread of probability mass across different outcomes. The hypothesis will be tested by comparing the performance of the integrated approach against a baseline using trajectory balance objectives alone.

GFlowNet Implementation

Common Components for Both Conditions

  1. Implement a GFlowNet framework for Bayesian network inference
  2. Define the state space as partial assignments to variables in the Bayesian network
  3. Define actions as assigning values to unassigned variables
  4. Define the reward function based on the posterior probability of the complete assignment
  5. Implement forward and backward policies using neural networks

Baseline Condition (TB)

  1. Implement the standard trajectory balance (TB) objective:
  2. TB loss = (F(s→s') * PB(s'→s) - F(s) * PF(s→s'))²
  3. Where F is the flow, PF is the forward policy, and PB is the backward policy

Experimental Condition (SubTB + MaxEnt)

  1. Implement sub-trajectory balance (SubTB):
  2. Define partial subsequences within trajectories
  3. Apply the balance condition to these subsequences
  4. SubTB loss = Σ(F(si→si+1) * PB(si+1→si) - F(si) * PF(si→si+1))²
  5. Where i ranges over subsequence indices

  1. Implement maximum entropy objective:
  2. Fix the backward policy as a uniform distribution
  3. This encourages diverse exploration of the state space

  1. Combine the objectives:
  2. Total loss = SubTB loss + λ * entropy term
  3. Where λ is a hyperparameter controlling the weight of the entropy term
